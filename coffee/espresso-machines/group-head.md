# Group Head Types Compared

## 1. E61 Group Head
**Used in:** Many prosumer machines (ECM, Rocket, Profitec, Bezzera, etc.)

### How it works
- Large, heavy brass group kept hot by a **thermosiphon loop**
- Includes a **mechanical pre-infusion chamber**
- Uses a **lever arm** to control water flow

### Pros
- Very stable temperature (large thermal mass)
- Gentle pre-infusion; forgiving to puck prep
- Easy to service; universally available parts
- Classic espresso feel

### Cons
- Long warm-up time (20–30 minutes)
- High energy use
- Slower temperature adjustment than saturated groups

### Best for
Home baristas wanting a **traditional, reliable** extraction experience.

---

## 2. Saturated Group Head
**Used in:** High-end commercial machines (La Marzocco, Synesso, Slayer)

### How it works
- Group head is **part of the brew boiler** itself
- Brew water temperature is extremely consistent
- Supports advanced features like **flow or pressure profiling**

### Pros
- Exceptional temperature stability (±0.1°C)
- Fast warm-up
- Great shot-to-shot consistency
- Ideal for light roasts

### Cons
- Expensive to manufacture (and buy)
- More complex servicing
- Less forgiving for sloppy puck prep

### Best for
Users prioritizing **precision, repeatability**, and handling of lighter roasts.

---

## 3. Semi-Saturated Group Head
**Used in:** La Spaziale, Nuova Simonelli, and similar machines

### How it works
- Thermally linked to the boiler but not fully integrated
- Heated via conduction or electric heaters

### Pros
- Good temperature stability
- Faster warm-up than E61
- More energy efficient

### Cons
- Slightly less stable than fully saturated designs
- Less common in home prosumer machines

### Best for
Small cafés or prosumers wanting **commercial reliability** without extreme cost.

---

## 4. Compact Home Machine Group Heads
*(Breville/Sage, Gaggia Classic, Delonghi, etc.)*

### How it works
- Lightweight group attached to a small boiler or thermocoil
- Often uses electronic or timed pre-infusion

### Pros
- Very fast warm-up (30–60 seconds)
- Compact and efficient
- Beginner-friendly

### Cons
- Lower thermal stability
- More sensitive to grind/dose changes
- Some use plastic/aluminium parts

### Best for
Home users wanting **convenience** and mostly medium/dark roasts.
