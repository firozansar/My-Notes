# What Is Pre-Infusion in an Espresso Machine?

**Pre-infusion** is the stage in espresso brewing where the machine applies **low-pressure water** to the coffee puck **before full extraction pressure** (typically ~9 bar) begins.

Its purpose is to gently saturate and settle the coffee bed to achieve a more even, consistent extraction.

---

# Why Pre-Infusion Matters

## 1. Reduces Channeling
Low pressure water fills the puck evenly, preventing weak spots where water could rush through and cause:
- Over-extraction in some areas
- Under-extraction in others

## 2. Improves Consistency
A fully saturated puck extracts more uniformly, leading to:
- Better shot-to-shot repeatability
- More predictable flavour

## 3. Enhances Flavour and Mouthfeel
Pre-infusion often produces:
- Smoother, sweeter shots
- More clarity (especially with lighter roasts)
- More stable crema

---

# Typical Pre-Infusion Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Range |
|----------|----------------|
| **Pressure** | 1–4 bar (varies by machine) |
| **Duration** | 2–10 seconds (lever machines may use 8–15s) |
| **Purpose** | Wet, expand, and settle puck before full pressure |

---

# How Different Machines Perform Pre-Infusion

## Pump Machines
Use an electronic valve or pump control to reduce pressure during the first few seconds.

## Lever Machines
Provide natural pre-infusion: water enters the puck slowly as the lever is engaged, before the spring applies full force.

## E61 Group Heads
Contain a **mechanical pre-infusion chamber** that fills gradually, delivering gentle early flow.

## Compact Home Machines (e.g., Breville/Sage)
Simulate pre-infusion using software-controlled low-pressure pulses or timed low-flow modes.

---

# What You Experience in the Cup

- More even extraction
- Reduced bitterness (from avoiding channeling)
- Increased sweetness
- Improved body and texture

---

# Summary

**Pre-infusion** is a controlled wetting step that prepares the coffee puck for full-pressure extraction.
By saturating the puck evenly, it improves consistency, reduces channeling, and generally leads to a sweeter, more balanced espresso.
